We address all major concerns with concrete Round~2 revisions and argue that FairRSFM fills a genuine Datasets-track gap.

\vspace{-0.28em}
\paragraph{Why FairRSFM merits acceptance.}
RSFMs are widely used for land-cover, crop mapping, and disaster monitoring, yet evaluation still rewards \emph{average} accuracy. Distant sites can share a biome while nearby sites do not, so purely spatial/country splits miss a core EO failure mode (\Rone). FairRSFM is the first reusable protocol that (i)~standardizes biome-aware subgroup reporting on GEO-Bench-style tasks, (ii)~exposes worst-group gaps that aggregate macro-F1/mIoU hide, and (iii)~ships closed-form diagnostics (DBR, BOLP) future RSFMs can be scored against without fine-tuning heavy backbones. That package---\textbf{framing + metrics + harness + baselines}---is what the Datasets track is designed to publish. Empirics will be hardened as requested; the need and design are already endorsed by all reviewers.

\vspace{-0.28em}
\paragraph{(\AC, \Rone, \Rtwo, \Rthree)\, What FairRSFM is (and is not).}
FairRSFM is a \textbf{benchmark protocol and evaluation resource}, not new raw imagery---intentional for this track: (i)~a reusable biome schema (14 biomes$\to$6 macro-groups) for \emph{any} georeferenced RS set; (ii)~standardized worst-group / NFR / $\sigma_{\mathrm{group}}$ / EOdd / DPM converting transfer into ecological group robustness; (iii)~a frozen linear-probe protocol attributing gaps to the representation, not fine-tuning tricks; (iv)~lightweight diagnostics (DBR, BOLP). Unlike GEO-Bench (``which RSFM is best on average?''), FairRSFM asks ``\emph{does} transfer hold fairly across ecological regimes?'' They are complementary: GEO-Bench ranks capability; FairRSFM audits reliability under ecological shift. We will reframe the abstract/intro and avoid overstating generality (\Rtwo).

\vspace{-0.28em}
\paragraph{(\AC, \Rone, \Rtwo)\, Multi-seed uncertainty and numerical hygiene.}
We fully agree single-run tables and small worst-group $N$ need uncertainty. All probes are evaluated across \textbf{$S{=}3$ independent seeds}; we report mean$\pm$std for Overall, Worst-group, NFR, and EOdd (Tab.~\ref{tab:multiseed}). We will also (i)~report exact per-group sample counts $N_g$ for all splits; (ii)~fix typos noted by \Rone\ (abstract: m-EuroSAT $0.836{\to}0.843$, m-BigEarthNet $0.497{\to}0.520$; remove residual ``0.800''); (iii)~define \textbf{ERM} (Empirical Risk Minimization) on first use. For NFR/std with few groups: report them primarily when $|G|{\ge}3$; always publish $N_g$; elevate \textbf{worst-group} when $|G|{=}2$ (m-SA-Crop-Type). On ``0.836 seems high'' (\Rone): strong absolute macro-F1 can still hide bias---we stress the \emph{gap} vs.\ overall 0.940 (NFR 0.104) and biome ranking, not absolute failure. Linear probing makes multi-seed runs a high-value, low-cost revision.

\paragraph{(\AC, \Rtwo, \Rthree)\, Multi-model coverage and HP protocol.}
Prithvi-EO-2.0 was a completed case study proving the protocol; we agree a benchmark must characterize an \emph{ecosystem} of models. Under the same frozen probe we will add \textbf{SatMAE} and \textbf{DOFA}, and release a one-config evaluation harness so further RSFMs plug in without reimplementing metrics (Tab.~\ref{tab:multimodel}). Showing that worst-group gaps \emph{persist or re-rank} across architectures is itself a core benchmark finding, not just a completeness checkbox.

\vspace{-0.28em}
\paragraph{(\Rtwo, \Rthree)\, Dataset choice, $N_g$, and macro-groups.}
The four datasets are chosen because they (i)~have public geocoordinates for reproducible biome joins, (ii)~span single-label, multi-label, and segmentation, and (iii)~show measurable ecological imbalance under a shared sensor family (Sentinel-2 / Dynamic World). Metadata attachment is deliberately simple so others can extend FairRSFM; the contribution is the \textbf{protocol + metrics + diagnostics}, not metadata engineering. We will add full train/val/test $N_g$ tables for all datasets (Appendix; prioritized over taxonomy prose per \Rtwo), a global biome map for non-RS readers, ``Why these four?'', and fix the L86 artifact. On $K{=}6$: the 14$\to$6 map is a stability--interpretability trade-off---many of 14 biomes have $N{<}30$, so raw-14 worst-group scores are sampling-noise dominated (\Rone's SE concern). Macro-groups pool biomes sharing broad spectral/phenological/hydrological regimes. Sensitivity $K{\in}\{4,6,8,14\}$ will report group-size histograms, worst-group rank stability, and Spearman rank correlation across $K$. Primary metrics stay on $K{=}6$; $K{=}14$ is a fine-grained diagnostic.

\vspace{-0.28em}
\paragraph{(\AC, \Rthree)\, Stronger baselines; BOLP positioning.}
We will include \textbf{GroupDRO} beside ERM/DBR/BOLP (Tab.~\ref{tab:baselines}). BOLP is intentionally lightweight for a datasets paper: novelty is a \textbf{closed-form, zero-parameter} null-space projection onto ecological group means in \emph{frozen} RSFM embeddings---no backbone updates, reproducible biome-direction diagnostics, and a clean representation- vs.\ loss-level contrast with DBR. Mixed segmentation outcomes already show honesty: biome can be both nuisance and task-relevant context. We present BOLP/DBR as \emph{illustrative mitigation baselines} that complete the benchmark, not as SOTA debiasers---the right bar for this track.

\vspace{-0.28em}
\paragraph{(\Rthree)\, Segmentation centroids; fairness metrics; inference labels.}
Centroid biome labels ensure \textbf{one grouping rule} across classification and dense prediction; we acknowledge ecotone/multi-biome tiles as label noise. Revisions: flag the limitation; report the fraction of tiles whose neighborhood crosses macro-groups (ecotone rate); sensitivity excluding high-ecotone tiles; optional majority-pixel assignment in released code. EOdd/DPM are ecological \emph{error-rate} and \emph{prediction-rate} gaps across strata---not claims about protected human attributes; we will rephrase them as group-disparity summaries under ecological shift. BOLP inference is realistic in EO: georeferenced evaluation uses the same coordinate$\to$biome lookup as preprocessing; if $g_i$ is missing, residualization is skipped and only $P_k$ is applied (L364--366)---we will make this path explicit.

\vspace{-0.15em}
\textbf{Conclusion \& ask.}
We will deliver (1)~3-seed mean$\pm$std statistics across all models and tasks, (2)~2 additional RSFMs (SatMAE, DOFA) under a shared protocol, (3)~GroupDRO, (4)~macro-group sensitivity $K{\in}\{4,6,8,14\}$, (5)~full $N_g$ splits and ecotone analysis, and (6)~clearer positioning as a reusable ecological-robustness \emph{protocol}. These fully answer the AC and all three reviews. Given unanimous recognition of the problem's importance, complementarity to GEO-Bench, and a concrete plan to harden the empirics, we believe revised FairRSFM will be a citable community resource and a strong fit for WACV 2027 Datasets---and we respectfully ask for acceptance after these Round~2 updates.
